Testing the first contact

Once you have a shortlist, the first contact should test how the firm communicates and whether the listing gave you an accurate picture. The directory says you can get in touch directly by call, email, or a quote request form. That gives you a simple way to compare responsiveness across providers without overcomplicating the first step. Ask questions that clarify process. You are not trying to solve the legal issue in the first message. You are testing whether the provider understands the matter, explains the next step clearly, and gives you enough confidence to continue. A useful shortlist is one where each contact point produces information you can compare like for like. Summarise the issue briefly and ask whether it fits the provider's practice. Ask what information they need before a first discussion. Note how clearly they explain the next step. Compare responses against the profile you read, not against assumptions.

The first message is about establishing a rapport and gauging the firm's professionalism. You want to know if they are responsive, empathetic, and clear in their communication. A firm that takes a long time to reply or gives vague answers may not be the best choice for a sensitive family matter. By testing the process early, you can eliminate firms that do not meet your standards for communication and service.
